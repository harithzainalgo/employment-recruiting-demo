The problem

Hiring for retail stores is high-volume and paperwork-heavy. Every new hire passes through the hiring manager, HR, a screening tool, a reference vendor, the Finance Director and payroll, and each hand-off runs on email and chasing. Good candidates go quiet or take another offer while their forms sit in an inbox.

1

Chasing paperwork by hand

Consent, nine forms and IC copies arrive blurry, incomplete or not at all, and HR spends days sending reminders one candidate at a time.

2

Offers stall in the queue

When a candidate declines or lets an offer lapse, nobody notices quickly, so the next-best candidate waits and openings stay unfilled.

3

Re-keying into payroll

The same bank, tax and address details are typed again into payroll, and the store manager often learns about the new hire on day 1.

What Opus solves

Opus runs the whole path from approved JD to day 1 as four connected workflows. It checks every document, sends every reminder, offers in score order and moves to the next candidate automatically, so people only step in for the decisions that are theirs: approving the JD, picking from the middle score band, setting salary and signing the offer.

Talk track

About 15 minutes. Each step says what to show and one line to land.

  1. Start with the job description

    Flow 1 · Publish JD

    The hiring manager writes the JD in the app, section by section. Opus pre-checks sections, salary range, wording and store region, then HR approves inline.

    “Opus catches the gaps before HR ever opens it, so HR only makes the final call.”

  2. The JD goes live

    Flow 1 · Job posting

    Opus generates the job post (a LinkedIn-style PDF in the demo) with the KitaHQ apply link, posts to Slack and marks the requisition live in the tracker.

    “One approval, and the posting, the apply link and the tracker are all done.”

  3. Scores arrive and Opus shortlists

    Flow 1b · Shortlist

    KitaHQ screening closes. Opus sorts candidates into three bands. HR reviews only the 70–89% band with interview notes. Everyone else gets a shortlist or regret email automatically.

    “HR’s judgement goes where it matters: the borderline candidates.”

  4. Forms and reference check

    Flow 2a · Daniel and Wei Ling

    Show consent captured first. Daniel’s blurry IC gets caught and reminded twice, then he’s cleared. Wei Ling’s reference comes back flagged and she’s marked Not cleared.

    “Nobody on your team chased a single form here.”

  5. Offer in score order

    Flow 2b · Aisyah

    Aisyah is offered first. The hiring manager proposes salary and confirms the store, Opus drafts the letter, the FD signs, then she signs in the portal.

    “The best-scored cleared candidate always gets the first offer.”

  6. A lapsed offer moves the queue

    Flow 2b · Mei, then Daniel

    Mei lets her offer lapse after reminders on day 2 and 4. Opus marks her Dropped and starts an offer for Daniel without anyone asking.

    “Openings don’t sit empty because someone forgot to check an inbox.”

  7. Payroll and day 1

    Flow 2b · Ramco and day-1 message

    The new-hire form goes into Ramco, the employee ID comes back, and the day-1 message reaches Aisyah with the store manager copied. Close on the tracker and the audit trail.

    “From signed offer to first day, nothing was typed twice.”

Objections

The five questions that come up most, with the answer grounded in how the workflow actually behaves.

“Is AI deciding who gets hired?”

No. Scores come from KitaHQ’s video interviews, and the bands are rules the client sets. HR personally reviews every candidate in the 70–89% band.

People still approve the JD, set the salary and sign the offer. Opus handles the checking, chasing and routing around those decisions.

“What happens when candidates don’t respond?”

For forms, Opus runs up to three reminder rounds, two days apart, and stops early once everything is valid. After round 3, the paperwork owner in HR follows up personally.

For offers, there’s a 5-day signing window with reminders on days 2 and 4. A lapse automatically moves the offer to the next cleared candidate by score.

“We don’t have an ATS. Ramco is payroll only.”

That’s the setup this demo is built for. The Candidate Tracker sheet stands in for an ATS, and every flow reads and writes it, so status is always current without buying another system.

“What if the Ramco integration fails?”

The flow doesn’t stop. If Ramco doesn’t return an employee ID, HR ops gets a pre-filled sheet from Opus to key the new hire in by hand, and the flow carries on to the day-1 message once the ID is entered.

“How do we handle candidate consent and personal data?”

Consent is the first step of Flow 2a, before any forms are requested, and it covers the reference check too. Every action, reminder and decision is recorded in the Opus audit trail.

Confirm data-handling requirements with the client’s legal or compliance team before go-live.
